Logistics Platform & Operational Transformation

Moving a logistics operation off fragmented and third-party workflows and onto one connected platform, where every domain shares a single data model.

( A ) The challenge

Each part of the operation lived in a different place

Shipments, customers, drivers, warehouses, fulfillment, cash on delivery collections, and accounting processes were spread across fragmented workflows and third-party systems. The same entity existed in several places with no shared identity, so a question that crossed two domains could not be answered without manual work.

The transformation was structural: the domains needed to become one connected platform on one data model before any reporting or automation on top of them could be trusted.

( B ) Architecture

( C ) My contribution

Data relationships, backend, and integration

Data relationships

Designed the PostgreSQL relationships across the operational domains, so a shipment, its driver, its collection, and its accounting entry are genuinely linked rather than matched by hand.

Backend & APIs

Built Python backend components and REST APIs that expose those domains to the operational applications consistently.

Integration & process design

Worked on the integrations that tie the domains together, and on the process design that decides where each piece of operational truth belongs.

This was a team platform. My contribution was in the data model, the backend components and APIs, and the cross-domain integration, not sole ownership of the product.

Scope of this write-up

Described at the system and architecture level. No uptime, volume, or efficiency figures are claimed, and no internal company detail is exposed.
