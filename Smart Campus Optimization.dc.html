01 — Problem

A fixed campus schedule ignores operational demand

Campus waste collection and internal transport demand change with building occupancy. Static schedules can produce unnecessary trips, energy use, cost, and emissions because the system is not responding to measured or predicted demand.

Objective. Predict building occupancy and use those predictions to dynamically plan waste collection, transport demand, route optimization, and sustainability reporting.

Feature preparation

Preparing calendar, building and weather inputs

Encoding and time alignment

Label encoders transform month, day of week, and period start time. Building names use one-hot encoding. Period starts are normalized to hourly values used by the encoders.

Data preparation and inference

Pandas and NumPy handle CSV data, missing values, building-by-hour tables, and occupancy aggregation. The Streamlit interface collects calendar, lecture, event, exam, holiday, and weather inputs for inference.

05 — Optimization layer

From calculated demand to a capacity-constrained route

Mathematical estimation

Daily occupancy aggregates are converted to waste quantities using predefined coefficients and base loads: Waste = coefficient × occupancy + base waste. Fill ratios are calculated from waste weight and container capacity. This stage uses explicit mathematical assumptions, rather than a separate trained waste-prediction model.

Threshold and greedy selection

Containers become eligible at 50% of capacity. Eligible containers are sorted by fill ratio; each is added only if the selected load remains within the 3,000 kg truck capacity. Greedy selection prioritizes fullness without enumerating every possible subset.

Weighted road graph

NetworkX represents Gate 5, the containers, disposal site, and intermediate road points as graph nodes. Haversine distances weight the connected road edges. Routes follow this road network.

Dijkstra and the distance matrix

Weighted shortest paths are calculated between stops using NetworkX/Dijkstra. Their distances form the matrix supplied to the routing solver; shortest-path reconstruction supplies the road-node geometry displayed on the map.

Capacity-constrained routing

Google OR-Tools models the collection route as a Capacitated Vehicle Routing Problem (CVRP): start at Gate 5, visit each selected container, respect the 3,000 kg capacity, and end at the disposal site while minimizing route distance.

Initial solution and local search

PATH_CHEAPEST_ARC constructs an initial route. GUIDED_LOCAL_SEARCH improves it within a five-second search limit. The time-bounded heuristic returns a feasible route within the configured search window.

Distinct algorithm roles: the greedy step selects containers, Dijkstra computes paths within the road graph, and OR-Tools searches the collection sequence. Occupancy regression and transport scheduling are separate stages.

Transport scheduling

Turning hourly occupancy into trips and headways

Demand and required trips

Hourly occupancy is summed across buildings. An assumed 25% participation rate gives transport demand. Required trips are calculated as ceil(demand / 350), using the configured capacity of 350 passengers per trip.

Capacity-based schedule

For periods requiring service, headway is calculated from the available period divided by required trips. The interface displays the resulting trip counts and schedule. This is a demand/capacity scheduling method, separate from the waste-route CVRP.

Interactive decision support

Streamlit brings together occupancy forecasts, transport schedules, route outputs, and sustainability indicators. Folium maps the original application’s optimized road route. The linked portfolio map displays a recorded example run.

Energy, cost and emissions

Transparent calculations with explicit assumptions

Collection-route calculations

The implementation applies fixed factors to route distance: energy = 1.8 × distance; cost = 0.705 × energy; emissions = 2650 × energy. Comparison uses a 1.844 km reference route. These scenario indicators compare configured calculations with the reference route.

Transport calculations

Transport energy uses an assumed 26 kWh per trip, followed by emissions calculations and comparison with fixed reference values. Participation rates, vehicle capacities, and environmental coefficients are model inputs and assumptions rather than learned predictions.

Project scope: developed through research and implemented as a practical planning application. The application connects forecasting, mathematical calculations, optimization, scheduling, and visualization in one decision-support workflow.
