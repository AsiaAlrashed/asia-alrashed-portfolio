01 — Problem

A fixed campus schedule ignores operational demand

Campus waste collection and internal transport demand change with building occupancy. Static schedules can produce unnecessary trips, energy use, cost, and emissions because the system is not responding to measured or predicted demand.

Objective. Predict building occupancy and use those predictions to dynamically plan waste collection, transport demand, route optimization, and sustainability reporting.

05 — Waste optimization

Network-aware routing, not straight-line routing

Demand layer

Predicted occupancy is converted into waste estimates and container fill levels. Containers at or above the 50% fill threshold are selected for collection while respecting a 3000kg truck capacity.

Routing layer

The campus road network is represented with NetworkX. Shortest-path distances on that graph feed the OR-Tools CVRP solver, so the route follows campus road nodes instead of drawing simple straight lines between containers.

Sustainability layer

Route outputs connect to distance, energy consumption, operational cost, and CO₂ emissions, linking ML predictions to operational sustainability decisions.

Optimization architecture

Campus Road Network → NetworkX Graph → Shortest-Path Distances → Selected Waste Containers → Truck Capacity → OR-Tools CVRP → Optimized Collection Sequence → Route on Campus Map.
