LLM-Powered Conversational AI & Fine-Tuning

A support assistant that answers from company knowledge, in the customer’s own dialect.

I developed and deployed an LLM-powered conversational AI assistant for customer and operational support. The model was fine-tuned with Hugging Face Transformers, Trainer, and Datasets, adapted efficiently with Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning, then connected to a Retrieval-Augmented Generation pipeline so every answer is grounded in company knowledge rather than the model’s own guesses.

( 01 ) The problem

A general model answers fluently, and often wrongly

Support conversations needed answers tied to the company’s own services, policies, and operational reality. A general-purpose model produces confident text but has no access to that knowledge, and it does not speak the dialect customers actually write in. Full fine-tuning of a large model for every update was not practical either, in compute or in time.

Knowledge gap

Answers had to come from company knowledge, not from whatever the base model happened to have learned.

Dialect gap

Customers write informal Arabic, including Iraqi dialect, so tone and phrasing needed adaptation, not just translation.

Cost gap

Adapting a large model repeatedly with full fine-tuning is expensive, so the update path had to be lighter.

( 02 ) Approach

Fine-tune for voice, retrieve for facts

The system separates two jobs. Fine-tuning teaches the model how to speak: tone, intent coverage, and dialect. Retrieval supplies what to say: the approved company content pulled at answer time. That split keeps knowledge updates out of the training loop.

( 03 ) Fine-tuning

LoRA and QLoRA, so adaptation stays affordable

Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning adapts a pre-trained model by training a small set of additional parameters instead of rewriting the whole network. QLoRA adds quantization on top, lowering the memory needed to train.

What was trained

A small adapter on top of a pre-trained language model, using Hugging Face Trainer and Datasets, with tokenization and training configuration managed explicitly.

Why it matters here

Fewer trainable parameters and lower compute means the model can be re-adapted as tone and intent requirements change, without a full training run each time.

What was measured

Evaluation and hyperparameter tuning were part of the loop, with model optimization applied before the adapter was promoted.

( 04 ) Retrieval

Answers grounded in retrieved company context

Rather than trusting the model’s memory, the pipeline retrieves relevant company content for each question and passes it to the model as context. Updating knowledge becomes a content task, not a training task.

( 05 ) Datasets and language

The dataset is the product

Most of the quality came from dataset work: preparing and refining conversational data so replies land naturally, hold a consistent tone, cover the intents customers actually raise, and respect dialect-specific phrasing.

Naturalness

Conversational data was refined so responses read as human replies rather than template output.

Intent coverage

The dataset was extended to cover the range of intents seen in real customer engagement.

Dialect adaptation

Arabic NLP work focused on Iraqi dialect, so the assistant matches how customers write.
