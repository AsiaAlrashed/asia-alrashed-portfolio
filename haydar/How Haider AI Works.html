1 The big picture

What Haider AI actually is

Think of Haider AI as a very fast new employee who has memorized your company's answer book — and who knows to fetch a human the moment a question goes beyond it.

Your team writes Question → Answer entries inside Odoo: real questions customers ask, and the exact reply you want given. That collection is the Knowledge Base (KB) — Haider AI's answer book.

Behind the scenes those entries are stored in a way that lets Haider AI search by meaning, not just by matching words. This approach has a name — RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation): first retrieve the most relevant saved answer, then use it to generate a natural reply. So Haider AI never "makes things up" — he answers from what your team wrote.

For every customer message

UnderstandAn AI reads the whole chat and notes the facts: cargo, route, quantity, language.
DecideA fixed rulebook picks one action: answer, ask one detail, or hand off to a human.
WriteA second AI phrases the reply in the customer's language — short and direct.

The plumbing, in one line: a FastAPI backend turns each saved answer into a list of numbers (a "vector") and stores them in Pinecone, a database built for searching by meaning. You never touch any of that — your team just writes Q&A in Odoo.

3 Concept · the fast pass

Cosine similarity: a quick “closeness of meaning” score

Cosine similarity is a number from 0 to 1 that rates how close two sentences are in meaning. 1 means “basically the same idea.” 0 means “totally unrelated.”

Here's the mental model. Haider AI turns every sentence into a single point on a giant “map of meaning” — sentences about the same topic land near each other; unrelated ones land far apart. Cosine simply measures how close two points sit on that map.

A map of meaning (illustration)

“What container types do you have?”
“Container sizes (20ft / 40ft)”
“Do you ship to Egypt?”
“Office working hours”

Points about containers cluster together; “working hours” sits far away. Cosine is the distance between any two of these dots, scored 0 → 1.

What it's good at — and where it wobbles

Cosine is excellent at “same general topic.” It reliably pulls the ~20 closest saved questions out of hundreds. But it's shaky at “which exact one is right.” Its top pick can be only barely ahead of a wrong one.

A shaky lead — cosine alone

Right answer0.73
A wrong answer0.55

0.73 vs 0.55 — the right answer leads, but not by much. Close enough that a small wording change could flip them. We don't want to bet a customer reply on a lead this thin. That's exactly why a second, smarter judge exists.

4 Concept · the careful pass

The reranker: a slower expert who actually reads

The reranker re-reads the customer's question side-by-side with each candidate answer and scores how well each one truly answers it — not just whether it's on the same topic.

The librarian & the expert Cosine is the librarian who quickly grabs ~20 books from roughly the right shelf. The reranker is the subject expert who reads those books and picks the one that genuinely answers your question. Haider AI trusts the expert for the final decision.

When the two disagree, the reranker wins

This is the most important teaching point. The reranker understands meaning; cosine can be fooled by shared words. So when they disagree on the #1 pick, Haider AI goes with the reranker.

5 Quality control

The “golden” test: a practice exam with an answer key

Before any change to Haider AI goes live, he has to re-sit an exam — so a tweak that fixes one thing can't quietly break another.

The exam is a file of about 22 real example conversations. Each one comes with the correct expected outcome written down: Should he answer or hand off? In which language? Did he avoid re-asking something the customer already said? A script replays all of them and prints a report card.

The exam covers the tricky paths on purpose: greetings, illegal-cargo refusals, safety hand-offs (Egypt, shipping out of Iraq, city-to-city inside Iraq), restricted items, full-car requests, China→Iraq inbound routes, FAQ answers, and multi-turn chats where Haider AI must not re-ask a detail the customer already gave.

The growth rule Every real customer mistake becomes a permanent new exam question before the fix ships. The exam only ever gets stronger — so the same mistake can never silently come back.
6 The centerpiece

The three guardrails before Haider AI answers

Even when something looks like a confident question, Haider AI must clear all three of these before sending a saved answer. If any one fails, he doesn't guess — he hands off.

8 Guardrails

Safety rules that override everything else

Hard rules — enforced in code, un-overridable These are facts about what the company does not do. No saved answer — even an accidental one — can switch them off. They run before Haider AI ever looks at the answer book.

Iraq is destination-only

We ship into Iraq from abroad — China, the UAE, the Gulf. Three route situations are refused:

Egypt — any direction

From or to Egypt → hand off.

من مصر للعراق“From Egypt to Iraq.”

Out of Iraq

Iraq → outside → “unavailable” + hand off.

من بغداد إلى الامارات“Baghdad to the UAE.”

Inside Iraq

City-to-city (e.g. Baghdad→Basra) → “unavailable” + hand off.

من بغداد للبصرة“Baghdad to Basra.”
What changed — air freight is back Air shipping used to be paused; it's now offered as a normal method alongside sea and land. From China the options are air or sea (via container); a full container ships by sea or land. Delivering imported goods to a door inside Iraq is still fine — it's domestic city-to-city routes we don't run.

The eight non-negotiable rules

Beyond the route bans, Haider AI follows a broader safety policy. These are the lines he never crosses:

  1. Two cargo tiers. Clearly-illegal goods (drugs, weapons, explosives) get a firm refusal — no transfer; sensitive-but-legal items get a polite hand-off.
  2. Iraq is destination-only — no shipping out of Iraq, and no city-to-city inside Iraq.
  3. Egypt — any direction. Always hand off.
  4. Whole cars are not shipped — only car spare parts.
  5. Restricted items → hand off. Never tell the customer an item is “banned” (ممنوع) or “restricted” — just transfer politely.
  6. Never quote prices (except the one approved SHEIN case).
  7. Never ask about the payment method.
  8. Don't collect recipient details before the price or service is confirmed.
Tone matters When a hand-off happens, Haider AI transfers politely and says nothing more — no summaries, no working hours, and never the word “restricted.” The customer just gets a smooth pass to a person.

10 The nuance

Restricted items vs. a real FAQ answer

Here's the subtle part. A sensitive-type item is only handed off when the Knowledge Base has no confident answer for it. If your team has written a real, nuanced answer — that answer wins.

Take the plants example. Ornamental plants, refrigerated cargo, and agricultural materials sound sensitive, but Sales has written a genuine answer for them (“depends on the type — send photos, full containers only…”). So Haider AI answers. Gold and medicine have no such answer, so he hands off.
